# What Happens to a Moment: Design Document (v2, branching architecture)

Working document. Replaces the earlier single-track draft. Structure: one hard branch at Turn 1, two differently-shaped early games, reconvergence at Turn 5, a shared spine through Turn 12 whose available choices and outcomes depend on what got built earlier. The game pronounces the ending; it is never chosen.

Format per turn: a news bulletin (dateline + 80–130 word report giving only what would plausibly be known at that moment, no interpretation), a direct question, and choices written as **Action → Direct gain → Risk → Creates**. Where a choice's public reception is genuinely uncertain, a **Certain / Externality** split follows: the certain part is the structural, predictable consequence; the externality is a probabilistic draw (positive / negative / mixed) weighted by earlier choices and by accumulated Moral Legitimacy and Independence. Not every choice needs one. Roughly six moments in the whole game carry real externality risk; the rest resolve on the certain effect alone.

Bracketed notes are design commentary, not player-facing text.

## TURN 1: THE BREAKTHROUGH

**LIVE · FROM THE PROTEST SITE**

"The resignation has brought more students onto the streets. Groups are still arriving, and we're hearing new demands from the crowd.

"But police have tightened access to the site. Food deliveries are being turned back, nearby public transport has been blocked, and officers say no new arrivals will be allowed after dusk.

"Police have also been filming students and recording names and college details. Some here are worried about what those records could mean once they go home.

"We've just learned that a senior official has offered talks, on the condition that the tents come down first.

"The crowd is waiting for the organisers' response."

**What do you tell them?**

**A. We won. Bring the tents down.**
Direct gain: Moral legitimacy ↑↑, perceived independence ↑.
Risk: Mobilisation ↓↓, leverage ↓. Everything after this depends on the Government's word.
Creates: A Government-assurance dependency (the type of assurance secured, if any, becomes the fuse for the "cases remain" crisis later on Branch A).
**→ Takes the player to Branch A: Demobilisation (Turn 2A).**

**B. We stay until the settlement is complete.**
Direct gain: Mobilisation ↑, pressure on Government ↑.
Risk: Public perception can turn either way; police restriction and attrition continue to bite; allies must decide whether they're willing to keep going.
Creates: Settlement, alliance and public-legitimacy dependencies, unresolved.
**→ Takes the player to Branch B: Escalation, Narrow (Turn 2B).**

**C. This victory has changed the movement. Widen.**
Direct gain: Political space ↑↑, mobilisation ↑↑.
Risk: Moral legitimacy becomes vulnerable, Government can argue the original demand was never the real objective.
Requirement: allies must agree on the new demands, and you need a mechanism for deciding them, immediately.
**→ Opens the Widen Chain below, then rejoins Branch B at Turn 2B (skipping the simple version of Turn 3B, since the chain already answers it).**

[Retired: the earlier fourth option, "call the alliance together," is no longer a standalone Turn 1 choice. Its content now lives inside the Widen Chain's second sub-decision, "who builds this with you," reached only through C.]

## THE WIDEN CHAIN (fires only after Turn 1C)

Three sub-decisions in immediate succession, the same night, followed by a live test. This is not flavour text between turns; it is the actual organisation-building minigame. Widening was never a single action, it's a doorway into deciding who this thing is, who's in it, and how it works, before the press conference at midnight forces an answer whether you're ready or not.

### Sub-decision 1: Bigger Than What?

*"You told the crowd this is only the beginning. The words are barely out before someone near the front asks the question every organiser dreads."*

**What is the new charter?**

**A. Education reform.** Direct gain: stays adjacent to the legitimacy you already have. Risk: a narrower ceiling on how far this grows. Creates: an agenda that's easy to defend and easy to outgrow.

**B. Student rights.** Direct gain: moves into due process, campus policing, protest rights, disciplinary powers, broader than education delivery without yet being a full political programme. Risk: campus safety and disciplinary cases are politically stickier than curriculum reform. Creates: your first real exposure to "law and order" framing from opponents.

**C. Young people's lives.** Direct gain: employment, safety, housing, discrimination, economic opportunity, the largest constituency you can credibly claim. Risk: this is no longer a list of demands, it's the outline of a politics, and a politics has to be right, not just felt. Creates: an obligation to develop positions on questions you haven't thought through yet.

**D. Democratic accountability.** Direct gain: the broadest, most politically resonant frame, the resignation as proof public pressure moves institutions, not just universities. Risk: the most exposed to the accusation that the original demand was never the real objective. Creates: the fastest route to the hostile relabelling later on.

### Sub-decision 2: Who Are You Asking to Build It?

*"The charter needs hands. The mobilisation that got you here was never yours alone."*

**Who builds this with you?**

**A. Expand CJP itself.** Direct gain: you keep identity and control. Risk: allies who helped create the victory ask why it's becoming your organisation. Creates: a founders-versus-allies fault line that doesn't go away.

**B. Keep the existing alliance together.** Direct gain: immediate capacity, the people already here. Risk: their party affiliations and histories become part of your political identity whether you chose them or not. Creates: the alliance-versus-independence tension, live from day one.

**C. Open the doors wider.** Direct gain: reach, well beyond who's currently on the ground. Risk: governance gets much harder the moment the doors open. Creates: a coalition you can't fully vet before it exists.

**D. Let the protest constitute the movement.** Direct gain: the highest democratic legitimacy available, since the people actually here decide. Risk: you're building governance mid-confrontation, under police pressure, against a midnight deadline. Creates: an assembly with real authority and no time to learn how to use it.

### Sub-decision 3: How Does This Actually Work?

*"Saying 'national youth movement' is easy. By tomorrow morning somebody needs to know who can issue a statement in its name."*

**How is it organised?**

**A. Strong central leadership.** Direct gain: fast decisions, a small executive, appointed coordinators. Risk: easy to personalise, easy to attack through one person. Creates: every coordinator's mistake is now yours.

**B. A federation.** Direct gain: fast growth, room for local difference within a common charter. Risk: local actions create national consequences you don't control. Creates: a coherence problem waiting for the first public disagreement between chapters.

**C. A membership organisation.** Direct gain: the strongest claim to actually representing anyone. Risk: expensive and slow to build, and your own founders eventually submit to a process they don't fully control. Creates: durability, eventually, if it survives the building.

**D. An alliance council.** Direct gain: you inherit organisational infrastructure immediately, since partners bring their own. Risk: CJP is no longer synonymous with the movement, the "front for someone else" accusation gets easier to make. Creates: veto power for your partners, in exchange for their lawyers and organisers.

**E. Don't formalise. Let it stay loose.** Direct gain: nothing built means nothing to defend, energy stays maximal. Risk: no reliable way to decide what the movement wants, about anything, ever. Creates: extraordinary reach with no mechanism to resolve the disagreement that's about to happen tonight.

[Design note: E is a real, distinct playable path, not a soft game-over. It runs hot through the early turns since nothing is diverted into governance overhead. It starts costing the player at Turn 8, which has no legitimate mechanism to offer, at Turn 11, which locks out the legal-infrastructure and coalition options, and at Turn 12, which locks out the independent-political-force and formalised-lobby endings. It can still land on RELEVANT if Relevance and Mobilisation held throughout. It essentially cannot reach THRIVED, and it's the path most exposed to SURVIVED or ABSORBED.]

### Live Test: 11:40 PM · First Disagreement

*"Representatives have been meeting for nearly two hours. One group wants withdrawal of all student cases added to tomorrow's demands. Another wants employment included. A third says it will walk out if party representatives are allowed into the coordinating body.*

*"The press conference is scheduled for midnight. Journalists are already outside asking who is now leading the movement."*

No question is asked here. What happens is determined entirely by Sub-decision 3, above.

**Central leadership:** The executive decides in minutes. Cases go on the list, employment doesn't, the party representatives stay in the room. Fast and clean. The group that lost is on record as overruled by CJP, not by consensus. They remember which.

**Federation:** Three chapter positions, no single answer to give the press by midnight. The statement that goes out is vaguer than anyone wanted, a compromise nobody actually drafted. It buys time and settles nothing.

**Membership organisation:** Nobody has been elected to anything yet, the structure exists on paper and the process hasn't run. The decision gets made anyway, by whoever is in the room at 11:40. The organisation's founding story is now that its first real decision bypassed the process it exists to have.

**Alliance council:** Partners negotiate a shorter list and a decision on the walkout threat, keep the walkout group or keep the party-linked partners, not both. Whoever loses remembers it.

**No formal structure:** Nobody can settle it. Two press conferences happen at midnight, saying different things. Wire services run both. By morning, who's leading the movement is a live question, being asked of the people who are supposed to answer it.

**→ Rejoins Branch B at Turn 2B.**

## BRANCH A: DEMOBILISATION (Turns 2A–4A)

### Turn 2A: The Protest Ends. The Protests Don't.

**SIX WEEKS LATER · ACROSS THE COUNTRY**

"Student protests have broken out across several states, with demonstrations over recruitment, examinations, fees and university administration.

"Several groups are explicitly referring to last month's resignation as evidence that sustained protest can force action.

"The protests are not being coordinated by a single organisation. Local student groups, unions and independent organisers are taking the lead, while videos and protest calls are spreading rapidly online.

"The movement that began here has ended its occupation, but the protests it helped inspire have not.

"Organisers are now receiving calls from students elsewhere asking for amplification, legal help and support."

**What does your movement do now that the protest has spread beyond it?**

**A. Become the amplifier.** Direct gain: Relevance ↑, independence held. Risk: you own no infrastructure, others become the face of whatever you amplify, and your centrality depends on being invited back each time. Creates: a dependency on staying relevant to the next crisis, not a guaranteed one.
*Certain:* relevance rises without new infrastructure cost. *Externality — positive:* a struggle you amplified early succeeds and credits you publicly. *Externality — negative:* a struggle you amplified turns violent or gets accused of opportunism, and your name is attached with no operational role to explain it.

**B. Build a protest-support network.** Direct gain: Organisation ↑, institutional capability ↑. Risk: needs money, staff and time before it's usable, a crisis arriving too early exposes you as unable to help despite the offer. Creates: real capacity for Turn 4A and later, mostly invisible to the public.
*Certain:* organisational capacity accumulates steadily. *Externality — positive:* a small early test holds, building word of mouth. *Externality — negative:* the first real test is a big one and exposes a gap right when it matters most.

**C. Return to education.** Direct gain: Moral legitimacy ↑, agenda coherence ↑. Risk: narrower constituency, the wider youth-politics space fills in without you. Creates: a clean, defensible identity at the cost of visibility. Low externality volatility, this choice is fundamentally about opting out.

**D. Keep the alliance together.** Direct gain: Alliance capacity ↑↑. Risk: independence perception ↓ immediately, questions about who's actually in the alliance start on day one. Creates: a standing coalition, activatable later (4A, 9, 11), and a standing target for the proxy accusation.
*Certain:* alliance capacity accumulates. *Externality — positive:* the coalition handles an early request cleanly. *Externality — negative:* a partner's affiliation surfaces publicly and the coalition gets branded a front before it's done anything.

*World event, fires regardless of choice, sets up Turn 3A:* one of the spreading protests either resolves peacefully, turns violent, gets ignored by the press, or draws in an established political actor. Which is more likely shifts with the choice above; the event itself isn't controlled by the player.

### Turn 3A: The Government's Promise

**UPDATE · THE CASES REMAIN**

"Six weeks have passed. Cases filed against students during the original mobilisation remain.

"Some young participants say they have been bullied and targeted online since going home. Others are worried about police records made during the protest.

"Organisers are being asked why students were told to leave before the settlement had actually been implemented."

**How do you enforce the agreement now that the crowd is gone?**

**A. Announce a date for another protest.** Direct gain: tests whether the memory of mobilisation still carries weight. Risk: if the crowd doesn't fully materialise, everyone, including the Government, now knows the threat has a ceiling. Creates: a precedent, either that threats work or that they're bluffable.

**B. Build a public case tracker and legal team.** Direct gain: converts old mobilisation into enforcement infrastructure that doesn't need a crowd. Risk: requires accuracy and staff you may not have; a wrong entry undoes months of credibility. Creates: BUILT_LEGAL_INFRASTRUCTURE, usable at Turn 11.

**C. Use the alliance and political contacts.** Direct gain: leverage, fast. Risk: the Opposition-proxy allegation strengthens, and it strengthens permanently, this doesn't reset. Creates: a debt to whoever's contacts you used.

**D. Negotiate directly with Government.** Direct gain: access. Risk: the opposite suspicion from C, why is Government still willing to keep dealing with you. Creates: GOVERNMENT_ACCESS, and a question that never gets answered.

**Then, regardless of choice:**

**BREAKING · GOVERNMENT RELENTS**

"Government has agreed to act on the outstanding cases, days before any deadline arrives. Organisers call it proof the movement still has leverage without occupying the streets. Others note that the political environment has changed since the resignation, and that an independent, occasionally troublesome youth movement may now suit more than one actor's purposes."

No question here. No choice. The ambiguity is planted as a flag and carried forward unresolved.

### Turn 4A: Somebody Else Gets There First

**UPDATE · RESOLVED, WITHOUT YOU**

"A student confrontation at a state university has become a national story after police used force to clear a hostel block.

"Within days, an opposition MP arranged the release of detained students. A legal aid network filed the court petition. A campus union coordinated food, shelter and legal referrals for those affected.

"Many of the networks now organising the response appear to have expanded rapidly in the months since the original protest, and were largely absent from it.

"Journalists calling for comment are reaching the university's own student union, not your organisation."

**When another struggle can win without you, what does your movement contribute that nobody else can?**

**A. Remobilise nationally.** Direct gain: proves you can still summon the street. Risk: a third mobilisation call gets read as a sign it's the only tool you have. Creates: nothing new, spends capital rather than building it.

**B. Join the people already there.** Direct gain: Alliance ↑. Risk: ownership ↓, Opposition-proxy risk ↑, you're now visibly alongside actors who already carry that association. Creates: a precedent Turn 11 will test again.

**C. Amplify and support them.** Direct gain: Moral legitimacy ↑. Risk: relevance may fall since you're deliberately not claiming centre stage. Creates: a reputation for generosity that doesn't show up as capacity.

**D. Stay focused on your own programme.** Direct gain: agenda coherence ↑. Risk: the wider youth political space finishes developing without you in it. Creates: a narrower, cleaner movement that may simply matter less by Turn 10.

**→ Rejoins the shared spine at Turn 5.**

## BRANCH B: ESCALATION, NARROW (Turns 2B, 3B, 4B)

*Reached via Turn 1B. A widened player (1C) skips straight to 2B after the Widen Chain and never sees the simple version of 3B below.*

### Turn 2B: Day Five, "You Already Won"

**DAY FIVE · THE OCCUPATION CONTINUES**

"The protest has continued. The crowd remains large, but the Government has changed its message.

"Ministers say the principal demand has been met and accuse organisers of shifting the goalposts. Television debates are asking why the occupation continues.

"Police restrictions remain in place. Food and sanitation are becoming harder to manage.

"Some allies want to stay. Others say they never agreed to an indefinite confrontation."

**What now justifies keeping thousands of people here?**

**A. Keep it strictly about the unfinished settlement.** Direct gain: moral legitimacy protected. Risk: mobilisation may decline as the objective narrows. Creates: a clean position that gets harder to sustain the longer nothing new is added.

**B. Publish a second charter.** Direct gain: mobilisation may grow. Risk: you now need an actual political programme, fast. Creates: the same organisational question the Widen Chain already answers for a 1C player; for them this reads as adding to the charter they already have, a much smaller step.

**C. Let the alliance determine the next demands.** Direct gain: alliance capacity rises sharply. Risk: CJP ownership falls, ideological disagreement enters. Creates: for a player with no alliance yet, this is the first time one exists; for a 1C player with an alliance council already, this is deferring to a body that already has standing.

**D. Escalate until Government negotiates.** Direct gain: pressure rises fast. Risk: repression and public backlash risk both rise. Creates: nothing new, spends capital built earlier.

*Certain:* holding the ground this long costs Political Space regardless of choice. *Externality — positive:* police restrictions (food, transport, filming) keep reading as heavy-handed, sympathy holds. *Externality — negative:* coverage shifts to "why are they still here," sympathy erodes. *Modifiers:* more likely positive if demands stayed few and concrete; more likely negative if they multiplied without a charter to organise them, which is exactly why a 1C player arriving here with structure already in place should get better odds.

### Turn 3B (narrow path only): Whose Movement Is It?

*A 1C player never sees this; the Widen Chain already answered it under real pressure.*

**UPDATE · THE GROUND NEEDS GOVERNANCE**

"Three weeks on the ground. A spokesperson makes a statement nobody approved. A regional group starts organising in your name in another city. The occupation has outgrown the people who started it."

**Who decides what this movement wants?**

**A. CJP leadership decides.** Fast, but personalises every future mistake.
**B. A council of participating organisations.** Legitimate, but ownership diffuses immediately.
**C. An elected protest assembly.** High legitimacy, built under active confrontation with no time to learn how to use it.
**D. Keep it open, let it evolve.** Maximum energy, no mechanism, the same fragility the Widen Chain's option E carries, arrived at differently.

### Turn 4B: The State Changes Tactics

**UPDATE · GOVERNMENT TRIES A DIFFERENT APPROACH**

"By now Government has learnt that waiting the occupation out may not work.

"Instead of repression, it begins separating actors. It offers talks to some. It attacks others as party operatives. It publicly distinguishes 'genuine students' from 'political elements.' Some allies are invited into negotiations; others are excluded."

**Can you negotiate without letting Government break apart the coalition keeping you on the street?**

**A. CJP negotiates alone.** Direct gain: Government access ↑. Risk: alliance trust ↓.

**B. Everyone enters together or nobody does.** Direct gain: alliance holds. Risk: no settlement at all becomes more likely.

**C. Send a jointly mandated delegation.** Requires the governance structure built earlier (3B or the Widen Chain). This is where whichever structure was chosen gets tested a second time, not by an internal dispute now but by an external actor probing the same fault line. **Locked, with an explanation, for a player who chose no formal structure at all**, there's no mandate to give a delegation that was never constituted.

**D. Refuse talks, increase pressure.** Tests raw mobilisation against a Government now playing for time rather than confrontation.

**→ Rejoins the shared spine at Turn 5.**

## RECONVERGENCE: THE SHARED SPINE (Turns 5–12)

Both players arrive here in different political condition. A demobilised movement kept its moral authority and lost its crowd; other actors have entered the space it opened. A movement that kept going has more capacity, or more chaos, depending on what it built, and has spent political capital and made enemies to get here. The next eight turns read differently depending on which arrival it is, noted inline where it matters.

### Turn 5: Build Something Between the Protests

**UPDATE · THE SCHOOLS CAMPAIGN**

"A participatory campaign asking students to document their own classrooms has produced an unusually large response.

"Organisers report thousands of photographs and written accounts describing leaking roofs, absent teachers, closed toilets and schools that exist on paper but not on the ground.

"Parent groups in three states have asked how to get involved locally. Volunteers who worked on the campaign are asking what happens to the material next: published, investigated, organised into local action, or taken to the departments responsible."

[For a demobilised player, this is how the movement gets back into the national conversation without another confrontation. For an escalation player, this is proof the movement can build, not just push. Same bulletin, different stakes underneath.]

**What do you turn this into: publicity, evidence, local organising, or a case to take to the people who can fix it?**

**A. Stories.** Direct gain: Relevance spikes fast. Risk: attention without durable capacity, doesn't survive the news cycle. Creates: nothing lasting.

**B. Evidence.** Direct gain: institutional credibility, the one thing a court, a committee or an editor treats as real. Risk: requires staff and verification, a wrong entry costs more than silence would have. Creates: EVIDENCE_CAPACITY, needed at Turn 9 and Turn 10.

**C. Local organisation.** Direct gain: durable presence in places never reached before. Risk: operational burden triples. Creates: BUILT_CHAPTERS, or strengthens chapters already built.

**D. Institutional pressure.** Direct gain: gets specific things fixed. Risk: the co-option question, are you doing the Government's quality assurance for free. Creates: a GOVERNMENT_ACCESS seed.

### Turn 6: They Give You a New Name

**TRENDING · A NEW NAME IS STICKING**

"A new term for the movement is trending across social media.

"Unlike the movement's original self-description, which emerged from young people joking about being ignored and disposable, the new label comes from its opponents and draws on an older political language used to associate dissent with anti-state activity.

"Supporters have begun reclaiming the term in memes and profile names. Others are warning against it.

"Students from several campuses say their parents are asking them to stay away from protests. Some organisers fear that a movement which once made participation feel irreverent and relatively safe is acquiring a much heavier political identity."

[This lands differently by history. A movement that stayed confrontational, especially one that never built formal structure, is far easier to paint as a threat. A movement that demobilised and spent months on schools has a moderate reputation to fall back on, the label is less sticky, but landing anyway is a bigger reversal.]

**Do you reclaim the label, reject it, or change what people associate your movement with?**

**A. Reclaim it.** Direct gain: core mobilisation intensifies. Risk: breadth and public legitimacy take a hit, apolitical participants start leaving. Creates: a harder, smaller, more committed identity, the shape Turn 12's independent-political-force option actually needs, at a real cost in reach along the way.

**B. Reject it aggressively.** Direct gain: perceived independence rises. Risk: looks defensive to some, doesn't stop opponents using the label. Creates: no real change, mostly holds current position.

**C. Drown it in service delivery.** "Call us what you want, here are 4,000 broken schools." Direct gain: moral high ground protected regardless of label. Risk: ideological clarity stays low, a real cost by Turn 8. Creates: a strong legitimacy asset with no answer for what the movement actually believes.

**D. Embrace explicit, oppositional politics.** Direct gain: agenda and identity sharpen. Risk: participation threshold rises hard, likely to shrink the movement while making it louder. Creates: a movement that has become a political project from here on, which Turn 7 and Turn 12 both assume.

*Certain:* Independence perception moves (up for B, contested for A and D). *Externality:* genuinely uncertain, and buffered by whatever Moral Legitimacy or evidence capacity was built earlier.

### Turn 7: Your Issues Are Multiplying

**UPDATE · THE AGENDA IS GROWING**

"People connected to the movement have raised concerns well beyond education: recruitment exam fraud, campus safety, caste discrimination in hostels, unemployment, and an environmental clearance near a university town.

"Partner organisations do not agree among themselves on all of these questions. A recent statement from one ally on caste discrimination was publicly disowned by another.

"Supporters are increasingly asking what the movement actually stands for beyond the original protest."

**How many of these become your issues, and how many stay someone else's?**

**A. Stay education-first.** Direct gain: credibility on core issues stays unmatched. Risk: adjacent constituencies drift to whoever does speak to them. Creates: a narrow, defensible identity, the same trade the Widen Chain's education-reform option made, now made again at higher cost.

**B. Become a youth movement.** Direct gain: constituency expands sharply. Risk: every new issue needs a position built in public, and early mistakes are on the record. Creates: an ideological burden that feeds Turn 12's political-force requirements if it holds, or public embarrassment if it doesn't.

**C. Build issue coalitions.** Direct gain: better outcomes than solo effort. Risk: shared credit, shared blame, the name stops leading headlines. Creates: deeper coalition dependency, useful at Turns 9 and 11.

**D. Follow issue by issue.** Direct gain: fast, responsive, no pre-commitment. Risk: no agenda anyone can summarise in a sentence. Creates: a reactive identity Turn 10 will specifically test.

### Turn 8: Whose Movement Is It? [Major dependency turn]

**UPDATE · A DECISION THAT ACTUALLY MATTERS**

"Founders, chapters and members disagree, this time on something with real consequences: whether to discipline a regional leader who broke publicly with national messaging. Whoever settles this effectively decides who speaks for the movement going forward."

**Who has the authority to decide?**

The available answer is whatever structure actually exists, generated from Turn 1 or the Widen Chain, not chosen fresh here:

**Central leadership exists →** Founders decide. Fast, and expected by now, not a crisis. Accountability stays low.
**Federation exists →** Chapters decide locally. The national message fragments a little further each time this happens.
**Membership governance exists →** Members and elected structures decide. This is where it pays off, an actual functioning process, not one improvised at 11:40 PM. High accountability, slow.
**Alliance council exists →** The coalition decides. Legitimate, but ownership is now fully diffuse.
**No formal structure →** There is no legitimate mechanism. The disagreement resolves by whoever shouts loudest. Direct Accountability cost. No stable answer carries forward.

### Turn 9: The Government Opens a Door

**BREAKING · AN INVITATION FROM THE MINISTRY**

"The Ministry of Education has invited the movement to join a formal consultation on the issues raised over the past year.

"According to sources familiar with the guest list, at least one allied student organisation known for its opposition-party links has not been invited.

"A ministry official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the government wants to engage 'genuinely independent' youth voices going forward.

"Some supporters are asking whether attending without your partners amounts to accepting the government's terms for who counts as legitimate."

**Do you attend, and if you do, on whose terms?**

**A. Refuse, stay outside.** Direct gain: clean, principled. Risk: the consultation happens without you, Government shows it can bypass you. Creates: the lobby pathway closes for now.

**B. Attend privately.** Direct gain: quiet wins on specific cases or policy. Risk: looks like co-option if it leaks, excluded allies see betrayal. Creates: GOVERNMENT_ACCESS without transparency, a liability if it surfaces.

**C. Attend publicly, publish everything.** Direct gain: transparency compatible with independence, the only lobby track that doesn't cost credibility. Risk: Government may restrict future candour. Creates: TRANSPARENT_ACCESS, needed at Turn 10 and Turn 12.

**D. Bring the coalition, make it collective.** Direct gain: individual leverage becomes collective leverage. Risk: no longer the most important person in the room. Creates: convenor status rather than protagonist status. **Locked without COALITION_CAPACITY.**

### Turn 10: Established Politics Learns

**ONE YEAR ON · THE OPPOSITION HAS LEARNED**

"A year after the protests that brought the movement to national attention, both Government and Opposition have visibly adapted.

"Opposition-linked organisers now regularly appear at campus protests with legal support arranged within hours. Several opposition MPs have built consistent profiles around youth and education issues.

"Government has begun implementing some of the demands first raised by the movement, crediting the changes to 'ongoing ministry consultations' rather than to any single organisation.

"Coverage increasingly refers to 'the youth and education debate' without naming the movement as its origin."

**Established politics has learned to do everything you taught it. What is your comparative advantage now?**

**A. Mobilisation.** Only holds if Mobilisation has actually stayed high. If it's declined through repeated use or an ungoverned early game, the response should say so directly rather than let the claim stand: *you believe this is your power; the last three attempts suggest otherwise.*

**B. Evidence and agenda-setting.** Only credible with EVIDENCE_CAPACITY and TRANSPARENT_ACCESS behind it. Claiming it without them is exposed immediately.

**C. Local organisation and service delivery.** Only credible with BUILT_CHAPTERS or genuine local presence behind it.

**D. Political representation.** Only credible with institutional access and coalition credibility behind it. Without them this reads as aspiration, not fact.

[Mechanic note: every choice here should be checked against what was actually built, not just what's claimed. The outcome text confirms the claim if the flags support it, and punctures it plainly if they don't. This is the turn where the game stops being polite about the gap between identity and capacity.]

### Turn 11: Your People Need Power, Not Attention [Capability test]

**BREAKING · MOVEMENT MEMBERS DETAINED**

"Several members of the movement returning from a district meeting were stopped and beaten by unidentified men. One has been detained by local police on charges organisers describe as fabricated.

"A protest outside the district collectorate has drawn attention but no immediate response. Meanwhile, an opposition MLA in a neighbouring constituency secured the release of a different detained student within six hours, through a direct call to the district administration.

"Family members of the detained organiser are asking what the movement can actually do for them right now."

**What do you do to get your own member released, today?**

The menu is generated from what exists. Nothing here is chosen freely; it's chosen from what was built.

**BUILT_LEGAL_INFRASTRUCTURE? →** Use lawyers and institutional action. Habeas corpus filed by morning, released through judicial order, no crowd required. **Locked without it.**
**Strong alliance (COALITION_CAPACITY)? →** Collective political pressure, mobilised through the network. Broader, harder to dismiss, requires the reciprocal trust to have actually been earned. **Locked without it.**
**GOVERNMENT_ACCESS? →** Direct institutional escalation through the relationship already built. Fast. **Locked without it.**
**Mobilisation, always available →** Return to the street. Works, eventually. Each repeat use makes "eventually" longer, and the pattern is now known to everyone watching, including the people deciding how fast to respond.
**Opposition MLA, always available as a fallback →** Ask them to make the same call that freed the other student. Fastest possible result. Creates a debt that gets collected later, and teaches your own members to call the MLA directly next time, not you.

### Turn 12: Elections Organise the Space [Final conversion]

**ELECTION WATCH · ONE YEAR LATER**

"With state elections weeks away, several political parties are actively courting young leaders associated with the movement, offering campaign roles, advisory positions and, in at least two cases, tickets to contest.

"Two prominent figures from the original core team have confirmed they are in discussions with parties. Neither has resigned from the movement.

"Some longtime allies, several with existing party affiliations, are asking the movement to formally back specific candidates. Others are asking it to stay out of electoral politics altogether.

"Supporters want to know, plainly, what the last year was for."

**Elections are converting political energy into power. What do you do with what you built, before someone else decides for you?**

**A. Put independent candidates into the election.** Requires organisation, mobilisation, agenda and independence all built and intact. **Locked without them**, a movement pretending to be a party is visible to the electorate as exactly that.

**B. Publish demands and negotiate commitments with every political formation.** Requires evidence, institutional credibility and independence. **Locked without them.**

**C. Campaign around issues, refuse endorsements.** Requires relevance, moral legitimacy and reach. Available to a movement that stayed morally credible even without heavy structure, the social-media-only path's best realistic outcome.

**D. Let leaders and allies make their own electoral choices.** No requirements, always available. High probability of political capital migrating into established parties one person at a time. This is where an ungoverned movement, and often a movement that never resolved the alliance-versus-independence tension, tends to land by default.

## MECHANIC NOTES (for whoever builds this)

**Certain vs. externality:** most choices resolve on their certain, structural effect. Roughly six moments carry a probabilistic externality draw: Turn 1's choice itself, Turn 2A, Turn 2B, the Government-relents beat (3A), Turn 6 (the relabelling), and Turn 12. The odds shift with prior choices and with accumulated Moral Legitimacy and Independence, which function as buffers, the same external event (a police crackdown, a leaked meeting) reads as repression to a high-legitimacy movement and as provocation to a low-legitimacy one.

**Capability gates:** Turns 8, 9D, 11 and 12 generate their menu from flags set earlier rather than offering four options unconditionally. A locked option should say why it's locked, not simply disappear, the player should be able to trace the lock back to a specific earlier choice.

**The credibility check (Turn 10 especially):** any choice that claims a strength should be checked against the variables actually accumulated. A claim unsupported by the numbers gets punctured in the outcome text, not validated by default.

**Branch history persists as flavour and as odds, not just as flags:** the same bulletin at Turn 5 or Turn 6 should read differently depending on whether the player demobilised, escalated narrow, or escalated widened, noted inline above rather than written as separate duplicate turns throughout.

## ENDINGS (pronounced by the game, not chosen)

The four endings are not a ladder from bad to good. They are four different destinations for the political capital the original moment created. The governing question: one year later, where does the political power the movement created now live? Each has a verb.

| Ending | Verb | |
|---|---|---|
| **THRIVED** | Shapes. | Pressures, sets narratives, produces change. |
| **RELEVANT** | Amplifies. | Campaigns and communicates, occasionally sets the conversation, but others increasingly organise and deliver. |
| **SURVIVED** | Exists. | Retains a diminished organisation or community; the political space has moved elsewhere. |
| **ABSORBED** | Transfers. | Converts its political capital into the machinery of established electoral politics. |

**Growth principle:** a movement can grow in numbers, organisation, attention and political power while simultaneously losing moral legitimacy, breadth or independence. SURVIVED can be a bad ending, worse than RELEVANT, sometimes worse than ABSORBED. Still existing says nothing about what that existence is worth. And ABSORBED is not synonymous with failure: high relevance, high mobilisation and a charismatic leadership make a movement more attractive to absorb, not less, which is why the game checks for absorption first, before asking whether the movement was strong.

- **THRIVED:** "You turned a moment into political power. The movement can still create pressure, but pressure is no longer the only thing it can do. It can put issues into the political conversation, organise people around them and make institutions respond. Other political actors have entered the space you opened, but they have not made you unnecessary."
- **RELEVANT:** "You kept the account. You lost the movement. You can still make something trend, embarrass an institution, bring thousands into a conversation. But increasingly, that is where your power ends. You remain a political media force. You did not become a durable political force."
- **SURVIVED:** "The movement is still here. The moment isn't. You still have followers, organisers and occasional campaigns. But the political space you opened is now occupied by people with stronger organisations, clearer constituencies and greater access to power. You did not disappear. You became smaller than the political moment you created."
- **ABSORBED:** "The political capital survived, but the independent movement did not." Two things vary underneath that line: which kind of formation absorbed it (an established party, or a newer one presenting itself as the movement's natural successor), and what let it happen (leader capture, alliance boundary blur, or simply that the movement became valuable enough to recruit from).
