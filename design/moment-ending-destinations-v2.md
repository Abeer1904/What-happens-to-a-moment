Yes. This changes the ending logic quite substantially. The four endings should not be understood as a ladder from bad to good. They are four different destinations for the political capital created by the original moment.

I would define them around one question:

One year later, where does the political power created by the movement now live?

1. THRIVED: the movement became a political force

Thriving does not mean becoming a political party. It means the movement has become capable of creating sustained political pressure, setting the narrative, and producing meaningful change.

It still has enough mobilisation to threaten action, but it no longer depends entirely on protest. It can set issues rather than merely react to them. It has built at least some mechanisms through which attention becomes outcomes: legal action, evidence, chapters, coalitions, institutional pressure or negotiated commitments.

I would therefore make THRIVED require all three pillars:

PRESSURE

* Mobilisation ≥ 55 or strong Coalition Capacity
* Organisation ≥ 55
* Has successfully forced at least one outcome after the original protest without relying entirely on an established party

NARRATIVE

* Relevance ≥ 60
* Agenda Coherence ≥ 55
* The movement has set or substantially shaped at least one issue after the original victory

CHANGE

* At least two meaningful capabilities built: Legal Infrastructure, Evidence Capacity, Local Chapters, Coalition Capacity, Transparent Government Access
* Institutional Leverage ≥ 50
* At least one of those capabilities has actually delivered an outcome

And:

* Actual Independence ≥ 50
* Not absorbed at Turn 12.

The ending becomes:

THRIVED
You turned a moment into political power.

The movement can still create pressure, but pressure is no longer the only thing it can do. It can put issues into the political conversation, organise people around them and make institutions respond. Other political actors have entered the space you opened, but they have not made you unnecessary.

That is the strongest destination.

⸻

2. RELEVANT: the movement became a social-media political force

This should be much more specific than our earlier definition.

RELEVANT is essentially the campaign/media trajectory.

The movement continues to matter. It can create hashtags, campaigns, stories, visual interventions, occasional calls to action and moments of national attention. Journalists still follow it. Political actors still sometimes respond to it.

But it did not convert that relevance into enough organisation or institutional capacity.

Conditions:

* Relevance ≥ 55
* Moral Legitimacy ≥ 40
* Social/campaign identity remains active
* Organisation < 55 or Institutional Leverage < 45
* Fewer than two strong durable capabilities
* Does not meet THRIVED
* Has not been absorbed

Likely pathways include:

AMPLIFIER
STORIES at School Theek Karo
campaign-by-campaign agenda
loose organisational structure
issue campaigning at Turn 12
high reach but weak chapters/legal/institutional machinery.

The ending:

RELEVANT
You kept the account. You lost the movement.

You can still make something trend. A campaign can still put an issue into the news, embarrass an institution or bring thousands of people into a conversation. But increasingly, that is where your power ends. Others organise the protests, provide the lawyers, negotiate with Government and convert the anger into political action.

You remain a political media force. You did not become a durable political force.

This actually maps very closely onto the trajectory in your original article: CJP potentially becoming a political-amplification Instagram page, participatory media platform, School Theek Karo-style campaign organisation, etc.

And importantly, RELEVANT can remain quite visible. It isn’t the same as SURVIVED.

⸻

3. SURVIVED: the movement remains, but the moment has moved on

This is where relevance has substantially diminished.

The movement still exists. It still has followers. It still occasionally campaigns. Perhaps it has an office-bearer structure, WhatsApp groups, chapters or people who identify with it.

But the political space has been ceded.

The Opposition, student organisations, new youth leaders, other social movements and Government have all become more important actors in the constituency the movement once activated.

Conditions:

* Not absorbed
* Does not qualify for THRIVED
* Relevance < 55
* Mobilisation < 50
* Institutional Leverage < 45
* No demonstrated capacity to set the national agenda
* Movement still has some organisational/social existence

The strongest mechanical signal should probably be:

Its relevance is declining faster than its organisation is disappearing.

It may have Organisation 50 and Relevance 30.

That is survival.

The ending:

SURVIVED
The movement is still here. The moment isn’t.

You still have followers, organisers and occasional campaigns. But the political space you opened is now occupied by people with stronger organisations, clearer constituencies and greater access to power. Increasingly, they set the questions and you respond to them.

You did not disappear. You became smaller than the political moment you created.

This is the giving-up-the-space ending.

It can have variants:

Organisational survival: office-bearers and chapters exist but political relevance has faded.

Campaign survival: occasional actions still happen but don’t shape the wider political agenda.

Core survival: the committed base remains, but Breadth collapsed.

So this is not “social media CJP”. That’s RELEVANT. SURVIVED is what happens when even the social-media agenda-setting capacity begins to fade.

⸻

4. ABSORBED: the movement’s political capital enters established politics

This one should become much more explicit.

It isn’t generic absorption. It means that an established political formation such as an AAP-like or INC-like party becomes the vehicle through which the movement’s political capital continues.

The movement’s leaders, organisers, constituencies, language or demands migrate into established electoral politics.

The important thing is that ABSORBED can emerge from strength.

A movement with:

* high Relevance,
* high Mobilisation,
* charismatic leaders,
* a recognisable youth constituency,

can be more attractive to political parties.

So ABSORBED should override THRIVED when the institution itself fails to retain that capital.

Trigger when:

* Leader Centricity ≥ 60,
* and significant leaders accept party roles/tickets or
* Turn 12D allows individual political migration,
* and Actual Independence falls below ~45 or
* the movement formally aligns with / enters an established political formation.

Additional probability rises if:

* Opposition politicians repeatedly delivered outcomes for members,
* alliance dependence is high,
* governance is weak,
* individual leaders are stronger than chapters,
* the movement has a broad political agenda but no electoral machinery of its own.

Then there should probably be two flavours of ABSORBED.

INC-like absorption:

ABSORBED
The movement entered the political mainstream.

The leaders, issues and constituency you helped create now have a route into electoral politics. But that route belongs to an established party. Your organisers campaign beside its workers, your demands appear in its programme and some of your most recognisable leaders now speak from its platforms.

The political capital survived. The independent movement did not.

And an AAP-like absorption could be structurally different: a newer political vehicle presents itself as the natural institutional home of the movement’s anti-establishment/youth politics.

Either way, the game doesn’t need to name the party. It can describe the type of political formation.

⸻

The ending logic therefore becomes much cleaner

I would reduce it to two axes underneath:

Independent institutional power	THRIVED	SURVIVED
Media/campaign power without institutional power	RELEVANT	eventually SURVIVED
Political capital transferred into party politics	ABSORBED	ABSORBED

But the actual state machine should check ABSORBED first, because absorption is about where the capital went, not how strong it was.

This is substantially better than making the endings depend primarily on aggregate scores.

And it gives each ending a distinct verb:

THRIVED → Shapes.
It pressures, sets narratives and produces change.

RELEVANT → Amplifies.
It campaigns, communicates and occasionally sets the conversation, but others increasingly do the organising and delivery.

SURVIVED → Exists.
It retains a diminished organisation/community, but the political space has moved elsewhere.

ABSORBED → Transfers.
Its political capital is converted into the machinery of established electoral politics.

I think those four verbs should actually sit underneath the ending system in the design document. They make it immediately clear what we are measuring.
